Ask an AI to write your storyboard

Copy the complete instructions, paste them into your AI, and replace MY STORY BRIEF with your story, duration, and available files. The prompt includes this entire format guide, every catalogued property and ID, defaults, timing rules, and a working full-control example.

Download AI instructions

In Map Studio, choose Upload storyboard in the sidebar. Upload a .json or .txt file, or paste the script. Preview checks the entire story and shows scene times, narration, layer counts, and gaps. Build timeline creates editable layers and a single camera track. Undo restores the previous map.

Use the explicit fields below for automation. Freeform paragraphs belong in narration or description; they are not interpreted as map commands. Narration is saved as text. Timed audio uses an audio list and selected supporting files. Imports use the current map unless project.mapBackground selects another source.

Full editor control

The script can use the editor's complete project and layer model. Download current map as script captures everything you have built manually. Uploading that script restores settings, geometry, images, visibility, stacking order, groups, split source clocks, keyframes, and camera samples. Existing saved .framemap.json files are also accepted. See the searchable option catalogue or download the complete JSON reference for property names, choices, and defaults generated from the editor.

Groups, changing paths, sunlight, and exact timing

Give actions stable id strings. A group action uses options.memberIds to list its objects; forward references are supported. Its origin and initial transform are native group properties. Group keys animate lon, lat, scale, rotation, and opacity. Linked attacks use attackSourceId and attackTargetId, so the attack follows moved objects.

{"type":"shape","shape":"front","path":["Paris","Berlin"],
 "options":{"frontSide":"left","width":3},
 "keys":[{"at":"00:30","path":["Brussels","Berlin"]}]}

{"type":"effect","effect":"sunlight",
 "keys":[{"at":0,"values":{"altitude":45,"ambient":0.5}},
         {"at":59,"values":{"altitude":2,"ambient":0.3,"warmth":0.5}}]}

Use animation:{"start":5,"end":12} or movement:{"start":6,"end":10} to position the layer's animation/movement diamonds separately from visibility. These times are absolute video seconds. Key entries can use at (seconds/timestamp) or frame (integer frame), never both. Native objectKeys, pathKeys, groupKeys, lightingKeys, animStart and split source fields in options retain their frame-based clock. The editor's limits and validation apply.

Images and timed audio

Press Add supporting files and choose your image/audio sources. Reference an image as "image":"asset:portrait.svg" in an object's options or the project's map background. The importer uses the normal SVG/image processing, embeds the resulting image, and includes it in project backups. You can also supply the editor's existing PNG/JPEG/WebP/GIF data URLs directly.

"audio":[
  {"file":"voice.wav","start":"00:05","source":2,"duration":30,
   "volume":0.8,"muted":false},
  {"file":"music.mp3","start":0,"source":0,"duration":240,
   "volume":0.2,"muted":false}
]

Audio times are seconds or video timestamps. source trims the beginning of the file; duration controls the retained portion. Both source bounds and video timing are checked before applying anything. An audio list replaces session audio; [] clears it, and omitting audio keeps existing clips. The motion preview plays imported audio. Undo/redo restores the scripted audio and visual edit together. Source filenames/settings are saved in the script; audio buffers remain session-only, so select the files again after reloading.

Motion throughout the scene

Storyboards use Cinematic motion by default. Scenes without camera instructions get a moving camera: traveling forces receive live follow shots, while other scenes establish their subject and continue a slow zoom. A short scripted camera move continues drifting through the rest of its scene. Objects receive gradual size changes across their timeline bars. Existing position paths, attacks, smoke, territory reveals, and damage animations play alongside this motion.

Choose Cinematic, Subtle, or Scripted motion only in the upload dialog. You can also set "motion":"cinematic", "subtle", or "none" at the storyboard, scene, or object-action level. Scene and action settings override the default. none retains your explicit animation instructions and omits generated motion. Use "camera":false or {"mode":"hold"} for a deliberate camera hold.

After checking the script, press Play motion preview or Play scene to watch the result before building. Scrub the preview to inspect any instant. This uses the same map renderer as the editor and export. To update a storyboard you already built, reopen Upload storyboard, select a motion preset, preview, and build again.

Camera zoom, orbit, and follow

ModeExample
Zoom in{"mode":"pushIn","from":{"target":"Berlin","zoom":4},"amount":1.2}
Zoom out{"mode":"pullBack","amount":1}
Orbit with tilt{"mode":"orbit","degrees":40,"pitch":35,"zoom":6}

Camera modes span the scene unless a shorter duration is supplied. In Cinematic or Subtle motion, a shorter move is followed by slow zoom and rotation. Follow always spans the scene and remains linked when you edit the route. Use object to select a force by its action name, or omit it to follow the first moving object. Follow accepts zoom, pitch, from, easing, and lockHeading. It does not accept a destination or duration.

For exact choreography, use absolute video times in camera.keys. Each key accepts at, a target or longitude/latitude, zoom, pitch, bearing, and easing. Keys replace preset instructions for that scene and remain editable on the camera timeline.

"camera": {
  "keys": [
    {"at":"01:30","target":"Berlin","zoom":4.5,"pitch":10},
    {"at":"01:45","zoom":6.2,"pitch":30,"bearing":20},
    {"at":"02:00","zoom":5.2,"bearing":-15},
    {"at":"02:29","zoom":6.5,"pitch":40,"bearing":35}
  ]
}

Resize objects over time

Use size for an object's base size, or sizeFrom and sizeTo to animate it between two sizes across the action. Animated sizes range from 0.2 to 8. These fields also work on scenes that generate units. For example, "sizeFrom":0.6,"sizeTo":1.4 makes an approaching force grow. Explicit size keys override the automatic size animation; add more keys for growth, shrinkage, and emphasis at precise moments.

{
  "type":"object", "unit":"tank", "from":"Paris", "to":"Berlin",
  "sizeFrom":0.6, "sizeTo":1.4,
  "keys":[{"at":"01:10","values":{"size":2}}]
}

After building, move the editor playhead inside the object's bar and drag its Size handle or change Size in Properties. Imported animated objects add a size key at that time. Scrubbing interpolates the size between keys. Choose "options":{"sizeMode":"map","sizeZoom":5} if you also want its apparent size to grow with camera zoom.

A four-minute video

duration: "04:00" sets a 240-second video. Every time is an absolute position in the video, including actions and keys inside scenes. Use seconds (90), MM:SS ("01:30"), or HH:MM:SS. Decimal seconds are supported and rounded to the nearest frame. Scene and action end times are exclusive.

{
  "version": 1,
  "name": "My campaign",
  "duration": "04:00",
  "fps": 30,
  "initialView": {"lon": 8, "lat": 50, "zoom": 4},
  "scenes": [
    {
      "start": "00:00", "end": "01:00",
      "title": "The advance", "type": "travel",
      "route": ["Paris", "Brussels", "Berlin"],
      "unit": "cavalry", "count": 8, "troopCount": 12000,
      "color": "#c75c4b",
      "narration": "Write the spoken story here.",
      "camera": {"target": "Berlin", "zoom": 5, "duration": 15}
    }
  ]
}

This short example leaves the final three minutes uncovered; preview reports that gap. The download examples cover all four minutes with an opening, advance, battle, control change, and aftermath. Their events are fictional.

Actions for precise control

Action types are object, army, travel, route, shape, effect, highlight, border, pin, label, scene, plane, and group. Actions inherit the scene's start/end unless supplied. They must fit inside the scene. Colors and options belong to each explicit action; scene shorthand settings apply to the automatically generated actions.

{
  "type": "object", "name": "Northern force",
  "start": "01:00", "end": "02:00", "animateFor": 20,
  "unit": "soldier", "count": 8, "troopCount": 12000,
  "from": "Paris", "to": "Berlin", "color": "#c75c4b",
  "options": {"size": 0.8, "objectView": "isometric", "formation": "row"},
  "keys": [
    {"at": "01:45", "values": {"damageState": "damaged"}},
    {"at": "01:55", "values": {"damageState": "destroyed"}}
  ]
}

animateFor finishes the movement or reveal early, then holds the result until the action ends. Without it, animation spans the full action. Object keys support size, objectAngle, objectView, and damageState (intact, damaged, burning, destroyed).

Common object IDs: soldier, tank, cavalry, musketeer, cannon, warship, jet, flag. Common effects: explosion, fire, smoke, cannonShot, musketVolley, arrowVolley, artilleryBarrage, meleeClash, battleSmoke, siegeFire. Shape IDs include arrow, front, zone, road, railway, and river. Unknown IDs are rejected.

Use options for properties such as size, opacity, easing, showLabel, label, objectView, formation, enter, exit, repeat, battleIntensity, highlightAnimation, fillAlpha, width, networkAnimation, and roadStyle. Unsupported fields and invalid settings produce an error before the map changes.

Locations and territory

Use names from the selected map or the built-in city list. Matching is case-insensitive. For other places, use coordinates: {"name":"Battle site","lat":23.8,"lon":90.4} or the text "23.8, 90.4". Latitude is limited to −85…85 and longitude to −180…180. Paths accept arrays of locations; text routes can use Paris > Brussels > Berlin.

A region can be written as {"kind":"region","country":"Bangladesh","name":"Dhaka"}; the importer loads its country detail pack. Use the region's exact key from the map inspector when the key differs from its display name. Historical maps use their own territory and region keys.

For an exact occupied area, use a highlight target such as {"kind":"polygon","name":"Occupied sector","path":[{"lat":50,"lon":10},{"lat":51,"lon":10},{"lat":51,"lon":12}]}. A shape action uses path and a shape ID. Country fills represent the selected base map; they do not reconstruct unprovided historical boundaries.

The readable text format

name: My story
duration: 04:00
fps: 30

[00:00 - 00:30] The advance
type: travel
from: Paris
to: Berlin
unit: cavalry
count: 8
troopCount: 12000
color: #c75c4b
narration: Explain what happened here.
camera: {"target":"Berlin","zoom":5,"duration":10}
action: {"type":"effect","start":"00:20","end":"00:30","place":"Berlin","effect":"battleSmoke"}

Field names are lowercase or camelCase exactly as shown. Start each scene with [start - end] Title. Use one field per line. Arrays and objects use JSON on a single line. Repeat action: for multiple actions. Comments start with # or // on their own line. The same fields work in both formats.

Storyboards support up to 100 scenes and 200 map layers within a 5 MB script file, plus selected supporting media. A file with errors leaves the current project untouched. Scenes may overlap, and preview reports overlaps and uncovered times. Layer visibility follows its timeline bar; add another highlight in the next scene if control should stay visible. The imported plan and narration are saved in project backups. Open Upload storyboard again to review the source; manual layer edits do not rewrite that source.
